# HCCS Enrollment What-If Analysis

**Will more students mitigate or exaggerate the financial issue?**
**Date:** 2026-05-19

---

## Bottom Line Up Front

> **More students HELP — IF** the school can add them without a proportional facility cost increase. Each new student contributes positive net margin (~$1,800-$2,400/student). But the facility bottleneck is the critical variable: growth without a facility upgrade is clearly beneficial; growth that triggers a major facility expansion could break even at best.

### Marginal Contribution per Additional Student

| Component | Amount |
|:----------|:-------|
| Marginal revenue (state + program) | +$9,700 |
| Marginal cost (variable: teacher, supplies) | -$4,500 to -$5,500 |
| **Marginal contribution** | **+$4,200 to +$5,200** |
| Less: allocated fixed cost coverage contribution | ~$2,400 |
| **Net contribution to surplus/deficit** | **+$1,800 to +$2,800** |

> Key insight: each additional student brings in ~$9,700 in marginal revenue but only costs ~$5,000 in marginal variable costs. The **contribution margin is ~48%** — strong by charter school standards.

## The Hidden Risks That Could Flip the Answer

### Risk 1: Facility Market
- Portland commercial lease rates have risen sharply post-COVID
- Current $585K may be below-market; renewal could force Scenario C2 numbers anyway
- A move mid-growth cycle (Scenario D) is the biggest financial danger

### Risk 2: Teacher Labor Market
- Teachers are the largest variable cost
- Oregon faces a statewide teacher shortage, especially bilingual teachers
- Growing enrollment = need more bilingual teachers = potential wage premium
- This widens the marginal cost per student and shrinks the contribution margin

### Risk 3: PERS (Pension) Escalation
- PERS rates are a fixed percentage of salary, rising statewide
- More staff (from growth) = more PERS liability exposure
- This is a slowly compounding risk, not acute

### Risk 4: Dilution of Fundraising
- Current success relies on a tight-knit, committed parent community
- Rapid growth may dilute donor engagement per family
- Fundraising growth may not keep pace with enrollment growth

### Risk 5: Program Quality at Scale
- The school's academic outperformance may partly reflect self-selection (motivated families seek out immersion programs)
- At larger scale, student demographics may diversify, potentially affecting test scores
- A reputational hit would affect enrollment demand

## Strategic Recommendations

### Do: Grow to 480–540 organically
- Highest marginal return: existing facility absorbs more students at near-zero fixed cost increase
- Prioritize K entry grades (fills from bottom, smoothest transition)
- Target facility utilization before committing to a new site

### Study carefully: Full build-out to 600
- Model says it helps financially (even with moderate new facility cost)
- BUT: only if facility cost can be kept under ~$700K/yr
- **Must** secure facility terms BEFORE committing to enrollment growth
- Consider: phased growth (480 → 540 → 600) with go/no-go gates tied to facility costs

### Avoid: Growth that outpaces facility planning
- Scenario D is the nightmare: forced into an expensive facility mid-stream
- Recommendation to the Board: establish a **Facility Task Force** to quantify:
  - Current building capacity (hard max students)
  - Lease renewal terms and timing
  - Market survey of alternative facility costs
  - Timeline for any build-or-move decision

### The big question for the Board

> **"Can we add 60-120 students without triggering a facility cost increase?"**
>
> If yes → growth is the single highest-leverage financial move available.
> If no → the financial benefit largely cancels out; focus on fundraising and cost control instead.
