## 1. The Governing Board Mindset

The single most important concept in charter school governance: **boards govern; managers manage.**

| Board's Job (Governance) | NOT the Board's Job (Management) |
|---|---|
| Set mission, vision, strategic direction | Run day-to-day operations |
| Hire, support, and evaluate the school leader | Manage staff or make personnel decisions |
| Approve budget and monitor financial health | Write purchase orders or approve individual expenses |
| Oversee academic outcomes | Design curriculum or select textbooks |
| Ensure legal and regulatory compliance; serve as **appeals body** for formal complaints after school-level process is exhausted | Intervene in individual parent complaints directly or handle first-line issues |
| Fundraise at the board level | Plan school events |

> **On parent complaints — a key nuance:** The board does have a defined **appellate role**. HCCS policies (AC-AR Discrimination Complaint Procedure, ACB-AR Bias Incident Complaint Procedure, ACA-AR ADA Grievance Procedure, KL Public Complaints) establish a multi-step process where the board serves as the final appeal after the complainant has exhausted school-level remedies. Policy BBAA also directs board members who receive complaints to route them through the formal process. The board's job is **not** to handle first-line complaints, but it *is* the board's job to provide a fair appeals mechanism. Know the difference.

A board that drifts into operations is a board that is **neglecting its real job**. If you find yourself discussing individual staff performance, specific vendor choices, or daily schedules — pause and ask: *"Is this a governance decision or a management decision?"*

> **HCCS Context:** The cheat sheet explicitly notes the board is transitioning from a *working* board to a *governing* board. This means some founders and early members may be accustomed to hands-on involvement. Respect that history while gently steering toward governance.

## 2. Five Habits of Effective Board Members

*Source: Charter Success Partners*

### Habit 1: Mission Alignment
Every board decision should connect directly to the school's mission. Before voting, ask: *"Does this advance our mission of educating for global fluency?"*

HCCS Mission: **Educating for global fluency** — Chinese/English dual-language, cultural adaptability, excellence.

### Habit 2: Financial Literacy
You don't need to be a CPA, but every board member should be able to:
- Read and understand a budget vs. actuals report
- Explain where revenue comes from (state funding, grants, fundraising)
- Spot warning signs: declining enrollment, ESSER cliff, low cash reserves
- Ask informed questions about expenditures

See [Financial Sustainability Analysis](../notes/financial-sustainability-analysis.md) for HCCS specifics.

### Habit 3: Governance Over Management
Support the school leader. Evaluate the school leader. Stay out of their way in between. If you have concerns about operations, raise them with the board (not with staff) and let the board address them through the school leader.

### Habit 4: Full Participation
- Attend ≥75% of meetings (per HCCS Board Agreement)
- Read board materials **before** the meeting
- Come prepared with questions
- Serve on at least one committee

### Habit 5: Ongoing Development
Charter laws, academic models, and accountability standards evolve. Commit to continuous learning — board training is becoming mandatory in many states (D.C. now requires annual training starting SY 2026-27). Oregon does not yet mandate it, but best practice says do it anyway.

## 3. The Board's Core Responsibilities

### A. Strategic Direction
- Adopt and monitor a multi-year strategic plan
- Ensure the plan has measurable goals (HCCS has FY24-28 Strategic Plan with three priorities)
- Annually review and update progress

### B. School Leader Oversight
- Hire the school leader (Principal/Executive Director)
- Conduct an **annual performance evaluation** tied to charter goals
- Set compensation
- Succession planning

### C. Financial Oversight
- Approve annual budget (before fiscal year starts July 1)
- Monitor financial reports monthly — focus on: **cash on hand** and **enrollment variance**
- Ensure annual audit per Oregon Municipal Audit Law
- Maintain adequate reserves (6-8 months is best practice; HCCS currently at 4.5 months)

### D. Academic Oversight
- Monitor student achievement data against charter goals
- Understand the school's performance framework
- Ask: *Are students making progress? Are achievement gaps closing?*

### E. Compliance & Legal
- Ensure compliance with Oregon Public Meetings Law (ORS 192.610–690)
- Ensure compliance with charter agreement and ODE requirements
- Maintain required policies and annually review them
- File required reports on time

### F. Fundraising & Community
- Each board member has a fundraising expectation (HCCS: ≥$3,000/person)
- Represent the school in the community
- Build relationships with authorizers, funders, and partners

## 4. Financial Oversight Best Practices

This is **the #1 area where charter school boards get into trouble**. Financial failure is the most common reason charter schools close.

### The Financial Dashboard

Every board meeting should review a **one-page financial dashboard** with:

| Metric | Why It Matters | HCCS Current |
|---|---|---|
| **Cash on hand (months)** | Runway if revenue stops | **4.5 months** (target: 6-8) |
| **Enrollment vs. budget** | Revenue driver — small changes = big impact | ~420 of 600 capacity |
| **Revenue by source** | Dependency risk (state, grants, fundraising) | $5.38M total |
| **ESSER remaining** | One-time COVID funds expiring — critical risk | Watch depletion |
| **Budget vs. actuals** | Are we spending as planned? | |
| **Fundraising progress** | Per-member goals | ≥$3,000/person |
| **Payroll as % of revenue** | Largest cost center | Target: ≥95% of BSD |

### Key Financial Questions to Ask

1. **Enrollment:** Are we on track for budgeted enrollment? If down by 5%, what's the plan?
2. **Cash:** What's our cash balance today? How many months of operating expenses does it cover?
3. **ESSER cliff:** When do COVID relief funds run out? What's the replacement plan?
4. **Facilities:** Facility costs are $585K/yr — the biggest line item. Is this sustainable?
5. **Audit:** Have we completed the annual audit? Any findings or recommendations?

> **Warning Sign:** If the board is not receiving monthly financial reports, or if reports are more than 60 days old, this is a **red flag**.

## 5. Academic Oversight Best Practices

### What to Monitor

The board should see academic data at **every meeting** — not just at annual report time.

| Domain | Key Metrics | HCCS Targets |
|---|---|---|
| **State Testing** | OSAS performance | Top 8% statewide |
| **Early Literacy** | K-2 literacy rates | ≥80% |
| **Early Numeracy** | K-2 numeracy rates | ≥90% |
| **Chinese Proficiency** | Gr5 Chinese IM | ≥70% |
| **Chinese Proficiency** | Gr8 listening/speaking IH | ≥70% |
| **Satisfaction** | Family survey | ≥80% |
| **Retention** | Year-over-year | ≥95% |

### Questions to Ask About Academics

- Are all student groups making progress? (Equity lens)
- How do we compare to our authorizer's performance framework?
- What interventions are in place for struggling students?
- Are we meeting our charter goals? If not, what's the improvement plan?

## 6. Meeting Discipline

*Source: BoardOnTrack, Colorado League of Charter Schools*

### Before the Meeting
- Board packet distributed **at least 5 days** in advance
- Includes: agenda, consent agenda, committee reports, financial dashboard, academic dashboard, strategic plan progress
- **Members read everything before the meeting**

### During the Meeting

| Time | Activity |
|------|----------|
| **10%** | Call to order, public comment, consent agenda |
| **20%** | Reports (financial, academic, school leader) — *summary only, not full presentation* |
| **50%** | **Strategic discussion and decisions** (this is the main event) |
| **10%** | Committee updates |
| **10%** | Executive session (if needed), adjournment |

**Key rules:**
- Start and end on time
- No surprise topics — if it wasn't on the agenda, it doesn't get a vote
- Public comment per policy BDDH
- Votes must record **each member by name** (per BDDG and ORS 192.650)
- Robert's Rules of Order (simplified) for motions and debate

### After the Meeting
- Minutes drafted within 2 weeks
- Draft minutes approved at next meeting
- Action items tracked to completion

## 7. Board Composition & Recruitment

### Ideal Board Skill Mix

A high-performing charter board typically includes expertise in:

| Skill Area | Why | On HCCS Board? |
|---|---|---|
| **Finance / Accounting** | Budget, audit, financial oversight | ? |
| **Legal** | Compliance, contracts, employment law | ? |
| **Education / Academics** | Understanding student outcomes | ? |
| **Real Estate / Facilities** | Facility lease, expansion | ? |
| **Fundraising / Development** | Closing the $1M gap | ? |
| **Governance** | Board process, bylaws | ? |
| **Marketing / Communications** | Enrollment, community relations | ? |
| **Human Resources** | Compensation, policies | ? |

### HCCS Requirements (Bylaws Art. III)
- At least **3** members reside in Portland metro area
- At least **3** must be parents of a current HCCS student (excluding PreK)
- Board collectively must have **financial and professional education expertise**

### Recruitment Best Practices
- **Board matrix:** Track current skills and identify gaps, then recruit to fill
- **Term limits:** HCCS has 2-year terms, unlimited — good for continuity, but ensure regular rotation
- **Onboarding:** Every new member should receive: charter agreement, bylaws, strategic plan, recent financials, board policies, and a mentor from the existing board
- **Conflict of interest:** Must be disclosed and recorded annually (per BBFA)

## 8. Working Board → Governing Board Transition

This is HCCS's current journey. Understanding it is critical.

### What a Working Board Looks Like
- Members personally handle tasks (event planning, social media, fundraising calls)
- Board meetings spend time on operational details
- The school leader reports to a board that wants to know everything
- Few or no committees — the full board does everything

### What a Governing Board Looks Like
- Members oversee, not do — they ensure tasks get done, not do them
- Board meetings focus on strategy, policy, and monitoring
- Committees do the deep work; full board acts on committee recommendations
- Clear distinction between board role and staff role

### The Transition Challenge
The board cannot flip overnight. Steps include:
1. Adopt a clear **board role description** and **staff role description**
2. Build **committee structure** — Finance, Governance, Academic, Development
3. Train members on the governance mindset
4. School leader must be strong enough to handle operational independence
5. Hold the board accountable — annual self-evaluation

> **For George:** As a new board member joining during this transition, you can be a valuable voice for governance best practices. When discussions drift into operations, a respectful question like *"Is this something the board needs to decide, or is this in the school leader's purview?"* can help steer the board toward governance.

## 9. Common Pitfalls

*Sources: BoardOnTrack, Education Week, Bellwether*

| Pitfall | Why It's Dangerous | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| **Micromanaging** | Undermines school leader, burns out board, neglects governance | Role clarity, governance training |
| **Rubber-stamping** | Board exists to challenge and question — not just approve | Prepared members who ask hard questions |
| **Founder's syndrome** | Founder board members struggle to let go of control | Term limits, board refreshment, clear succession |
| **Financial blindness** | Board doesn't understand finances until it's too late | Monthly dashboard, finance committee, treasurer who is financially literate |
| **Meeting myopia** | Board only meets, never evaluates itself | Annual self-assessment, strategic retreat |
| **Friendship board** | Members are friends who avoid conflict | Diverse perspectives, board norms, regular evaluation |
| **Ignoring the charter** | Board forgets what it promised the authorizer | Annual charter review, every member reads the charter |
| **One-dimensional board** | Everyone has same background/ perspective | Skill matrix, targeted recruitment |

## 10. Checklist for HCCS Board Members

### Onboarding Checklist
- [ ] Read the **Charter Agreement** (2023-2028) — understand what HCCS promised the authorizer
- [ ] Read the **Bylaws** in full
- [ ] Read the **Strategic Plan** (FY24-28) — know the three priorities and targets
- [ ] Read **Board of Directors Agreement** and sign
- [ ] Read **Ethics / Conflicts of Interest Policy** (BBFA) and disclose any conflicts
- [ ] Review **recent financial statements** — budget, actuals, audit
- [ ] Review **recent board meeting minutes** — last 3-6 months
- [ ] Visit the **school** and observe classes
- [ ] Meet the **school leader** one-on-one
- [ ] Meet the **board chair** one-on-one

### Every Meeting Checklist
- [ ] Materials read before the meeting
- [ ] Financial dashboard reviewed — cash, enrollment, budget variance noted
- [ ] Academic dashboard reviewed — OSAS, literacy, numeracy, Chinese proficiency
- [ ] Strategic plan progress checked
- [ ] Questions prepared
- [ ] Conflicts of interest declared if applicable

### Annual Checklist
- [ ] Board self-evaluation completed
- [ ] School leader evaluation completed
- [ ] Strategic plan progress reviewed and updated
- [ ] Fundraising commitment met (≥$3,000)
- [ ] Conflict of interest disclosure updated
- [ ] Board skill matrix reviewed; recruitment gaps identified
- [ ] At least one governance training attended
