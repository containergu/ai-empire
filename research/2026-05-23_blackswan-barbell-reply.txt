Dane,

You're exactly right — and that's actually the more interesting question than the paper itself. What happens when the "safe" asset isn't safe anymore? 2022 answered that pretty brutally.

Your instinct about flipping it is worth exploring. A "reverse barbell" — long OTM puts on the Mag 7 (or QQQ) + short-duration Treasuries or TIPS — is essentially a tail-risk hedge for the regime we're actually in. The problem is execution: OTM puts decay fast, and you'd need to get the timing right on both legs. That's a lot harder than the original paper's set-it-and-forget-it with deep ITM calls.

The cleaner version might be pairing short-duration TIPS (or floating rate notes) with long VIX calls or put spreads on QQQ — you get the bond floor without duration risk, and the tail hedge is cheaper than holding puts continuously.

But honestly, nobody has cracked this yet in a systematic way. The original paper worked because the regime was stable. Your idea works in theory but would get eaten alive by theta in practice.
